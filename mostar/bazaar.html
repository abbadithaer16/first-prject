Kujundžiluk and the old town

The bazaar

Stone lanes, mosque courtyards, copper stalls and riverside coffee, all within a short walk of Stari Most.

The Ottoman-era quarter climbs both banks of the Neretva. Most of it is pedestrian, cobbled and steep in places, so it rewards a slow pace and comfortable shoes.

Stops along the lanes.

Seven places within ten minutes of the bridge, from workshops to mosques to a room of photographs that asks for some quiet.

  • Bazaar streetKujundžiluk

    The old bazaar lane runs up from the bridge along the east bank. It is named after the goldsmiths, the kujundžije, who once worked here. Today it is lined with copper workshops, rugs, coffee sets and souvenirs.

    Good to know: it is busiest from late morning to mid-afternoon. Come early to see the workshops open.

  • ViewpointKoski Mehmed Pasha Mosque

    An early 17th-century mosque on the east bank, a few minutes from the bridge. Its riverside terrace and minaret give the classic view back toward Stari Most and the river.

    Good to know: there is an entry fee for the courtyard and the minaret.

  • MosqueKarađoz Bey Mosque

    A 16th-century mosque on the east bank and one of the most important Ottoman buildings in Herzegovina, with a shaded courtyard and fountain.

    Good to know: visits pause during prayer times.

  • Ottoman houseKajtaz House

    A preserved residential house showing Mostar's Ottoman layers: a walled courtyard, separate rooms for family and guests, and the furniture of everyday life.

    Good to know: it is a small house. Visits are short and unhurried.

  • Ottoman houseMuslibegović House

    A larger family residence from the Ottoman period, now part museum and part guesthouse, around a quiet garden courtyard.

    Good to know: pair it with Kajtaz House to compare a modest home with a grand one.

  • Small bridgeCrooked Bridge

    Kriva ćuprija crosses the Radobolja stream near where it meets the Neretva. It is said to have been a rehearsal for the great arch of Stari Most.

    Good to know: the terraces around it are among the calmest in the old town.

  • MuseumWar Photo Exhibition

    Photographs from the war in Bosnia and Herzegovina in the 1990s, shown close to the bridge. A compact, moving stop for context on the city's recent history.

    Good to know: allow an hour, and some time afterwards.

Coffee first, then lunch.

Eat on a terrace over the river at least once, and drink your coffee slowly. Nobody here is in a hurry.

Bosnian coffee
Boiled in a long-handled džezva and poured into small cups, usually served with a sugar cube and a piece of rahat lokum.
Ćevapi
Small grilled minced-meat sausages in warm somun flatbread, with raw onion and kajmak, a soft clotted cream.
Burek and pita
Spiralled filo pies. Burek is always meat; with cheese it is sirnica, with spinach zeljanica, with potato krompiruša.
Tufahija
A whole poached apple filled with walnuts and topped with cream.
Žilavka and Blatina
The white and the red grape native to Herzegovina. Order a glass of each and compare.

Old town notes.

Small things that make a day in the lanes easier.

Shoes
The cobbles and the bridge are polished smooth. Wear soles with grip, especially after rain.
Mosques
Cover shoulders and knees, take your shoes off at the door, and avoid prayer times. Some mosques charge a small entry fee.
Heat
In July and August the stone lanes hold the heat by early afternoon. Walk in the morning and save the river terraces for the evening.
Money
Workshops and small cafés often prefer cash in convertible marks (KM).
Photographs
Ask before photographing people at work in the workshops.

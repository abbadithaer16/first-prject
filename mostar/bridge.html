The Old Bridge, completed in 1566

Stari Most

Mostar is named after this crossing. The mostari were the bridge keepers who watched over it from the towers at either end.

A single stone arch has carried people over the Neretva since 1566. It fell in 1993 and was rebuilt stone by stone, and it is still the place the whole city turns to face.

Four and a half centuries, one arch.

From an Ottoman commission to a World Heritage site, by way of a war that brought it down.

1557

  1. 1557Work begins

    Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent orders a stone bridge to replace the older wooden crossing. The design is by Mimar Hayruddin, a student of the great architect Mimar Sinan.

  2. 1566The arch closes

    After nine years of work the bridge is finished. Its slender, high arch was one of the widest stone spans of its time.

  3. 9 November 1993The bridge falls

    During the war in Bosnia and Herzegovina, the bridge collapses into the Neretva after sustained shelling.

  4. 23 July 2004Rebuilt and reopened

    Builders work with the original techniques, cutting new blocks of the local limestone, tenelija, and lifting surviving pieces from the river. The bridge opens again to crowds on both banks.

  5. 2005World Heritage

    UNESCO inscribes the Old Bridge Area of the Old City of Mostar on the World Heritage List.

The jump.

Divers from the local club leap from the top of the arch into the Neretva, which stays cold even in August. A diver will often wait on the parapet while the crowd collects enough money to make the jump worth it.

A traditional diving competition is held every summer, usually in late July. For the full height of the fall, watch from the riverbank below.

Please don't try it yourself. The club's divers train for years before they jump.

Where to see it from.

The bridge looks different from every bank, terrace and minaret. These four views are worth the walk.

  • The Koski Mehmed Pasha minaret

    Climb the narrow stairs for the view on most postcards: the arch, both towers and the green river below.

    Good to know: there is an entry fee, and the stairs are steep and tight.

  • The riverbank below

    Steps near the bridge lead down to the rocks and pebbles at the water's edge, where the arch shows its full height.

    Good to know: the rocks are smooth and the current is strong. Keep out of the water below the bridge.

  • The Crooked Bridge

    A short walk up the Radobolja stream, a much smaller stone arch that is said to have been a rehearsal for the big one.

    Good to know: the terraces around it are a quiet place for coffee.

  • On the bridge at first light

    Before the day's visitors arrive the deck is empty and the stone is cool. It is the only time to stand at the top of the arch alone.

    Good to know: wear shoes with grip. The stone is polished smooth by centuries of feet.

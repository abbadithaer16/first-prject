Walks, trains and day trips

Routes

Mostar is small enough to cross on foot in an afternoon, and well placed for the valley around it.

Start with the old town walk. Then use the city as a base for a cave spring, a hill village and a ring of waterfalls, all within an hour's drive, or take the train north to Sarajevo.

The old town walk.

About two hours at an easy pace, including the minaret climb. Mostly pedestrian, with cobbles and some steep steps.

  1. Spanish Square

    Start on the west bank among the Austro-Hungarian buildings, including the striped facade of the Gymnasium.

  2. Crooked Bridge

    Head down toward the river and the Radobolja stream, where a small stone arch crosses the water.

  3. Stari Most

    Cross slowly. Stop at the top of the arch to look up and down the Neretva.

  4. Kujundžiluk

    Follow the old bazaar lane along the east bank, past the copper workshops.

  5. Koski Mehmed Pasha Mosque

    Climb the minaret for the classic view back to the bridge.

  6. Karađoz Bey Mosque

    Finish in the shaded courtyard of this 16th-century mosque, a short walk further into the east bank.

Out along the valley.

Four days out from Mostar. Choose one to see where it lies.

  • A dervish house at the foot of a cliff, where the Buna river rises from a cave as one of the largest karst springs in Europe.Allow half a day. Riverside terraces sit right beside the spring.

  • A walled Ottoman village climbing a hillside above the Neretva, with a mosque, stone houses and a fortress tower at the top.The paths are steep. Go early or late in summer.

  • A wide horseshoe of waterfalls on the Trebižat river, falling into a green pool.Many visitors swim there in summer. Check the current rules and entry fee on arrival.

  • The railway follows the Neretva north through Jablanica and Konjic, through gorges and along lakes, in about two hours.Trains are infrequent, so check the current timetable before you plan the day.

Getting here.

Most visitors arrive overland, from Sarajevo or from the Croatian coast.

By train
From Sarajevo along the Neretva valley, in about two hours. It is the most scenic way in.
By bus
Regular coaches run from Sarajevo, and from Split and Dubrovnik in Croatia. Allow a few hours from the coast, and carry your passport for the border.
By air
Mostar's small airport has a limited, mostly seasonal timetable. Sarajevo, Split and Dubrovnik have more connections.
Around town
On foot. The old town is pedestrian, and most sights are within twenty minutes' walk of the bridge.

When to go.

Mostar is one of the warmest cities in the country. The shoulder seasons are the sweet spot.

SpringApril to June
Green hills, a full river and comfortable walking weather.
SummerJuly and August
Hot, often above 35 °C, and busy at midday. The season for the diving competition.
AutumnSeptember and October
Warm days, softer light and thinner crowds on the bridge.
WinterNovember to March
Quiet and cool, with shorter opening hours in some places.

Before you go.

The practical details, in one place.

Currency
The convertible mark (KM, or BAM). Cards are common in hotels and restaurants; carry cash for stalls and small cafés.
Language
Bosnian, Croatian and Serbian are all spoken. English is widely understood in the old town.
Power
230 V, with European two-pin plugs (types C and F).

Outside the city

Stay on paved roads and marked paths in the countryside. Some rural areas of Bosnia and Herzegovina still contain landmines from the 1990s war. Never enter areas marked with red warning signs.

History

13 May 1950. Silverstone.

The first round of the new World Drivers’ Championship ran on a former wartime airfield in England. Giuseppe Farina won it in an Alfa Romeo 158 and, by the end of the season, became the first world champion.

The race weekend

Three days, one result.

  1. FridayPractice 1 and 2. Teams test set-ups, tyres and race pace.
  2. SaturdayPractice 3, then qualifying decides the starting order.
  3. SundayThe Grand Prix: about 305 km, with a two-hour limit.

On Sprint weekends, Friday has one practice session and Sprint Qualifying, and Saturday adds a 100 km Sprint race before qualifying.

The race weekend

Qualifying is a knockout.

Three timed sessions, each shorter than the last. The slowest drivers drop out of Q1 and Q2; the fastest ten fight for pole position in Q3.

The race weekend

Sunday is about strategy.

Pirelli brings three dry compounds to each race. In a dry race every driver must use at least two of them, so every car stops at least once. A good pit stop keeps the car stationary for under three seconds.

Cars and technology

Half combustion, half electric.

The 2026 power unit keeps a 1.6-litre turbocharged V6, but drops the complex heat-recovery generator. In its place, the electric motor’s output almost triples, giving a roughly even split between combustion and electric power.

Cars and technology

Air is the invisible opponent.

Wings and the floor press the car into the track so it can corner faster, but that grip costs drag. Since 2026, wing elements move: closed for grip in corners, open for speed on the straights.

Corner mode: wings closed, maximum downforce.

Cars and technology

Built to bring the driver home.

After the deaths of Roland Ratzenberger and Ayrton Senna at Imola in 1994, the sport rebuilt its approach to safety: stronger crash structures, higher cockpit sides, wheel tethers and, from 2018, the titanium halo.
